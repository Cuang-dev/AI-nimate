The $600B Chasm

🔍 Zoom
Everyone calls AI a bubble, but almost nobody explains what is actually inflating. Between 2025 and 2026, Microsoft, Meta, Google, and Amazon are spending over three hundred and twenty billion dollars a year on AI data centers. Yet according to Sequoia's famous six-hundred-billion-dollar formula, all pure-play AI software today earns less than forty-five billion—leaving a massive five-hundred-billion-dollar revenue chasm.
BEAT 02 00:26.3 – 00:47.6 21.27s

Round-Trip Revenue

🔍 Zoom
Why does cloud revenue look so booming anyway? Because of a Wall Street illusion called round-tripping. Hyperscalers invest tens of billions into AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic—not just in cash, but in cloud compute credits. When those startups burn those credits training models on Azure or AWS, Big Tech books its own investment right back as cloud revenue.

The Depreciation Clock

🔍 Zoom
The real time bomb sits on the balance sheet. Tech giants stretch their server depreciation schedules to six full years to keep quarterly profits looking high. But Nvidia launches a new GPU architecture every twelve to twenty-four months. When Blackwell and Rubin make older chips too power-hungry to run economically, hundreds of billions in silicon face sudden write-downs.

2000 Telecom vs 2026 AI

🔍 Zoom
Is this just the year-two-thousand telecom crash all over again? Not quite. In two thousand, telecoms used junk debt to lay dark fiber cables that lasted twenty-five years in the ground. Today's tech giants are funding AI out of massive cash flows—but they are buying fast-rotting GPUs that lose value in just three years.

What Pops vs Survives

🔍 Zoom
So what actually pops when the AI bubble deflates? Overvalued wrapper startups selling generic chatbots on rented compute will get wiped out as model costs fall toward zero. Meanwhile, the real winners—power grid infrastructure and deep workflow automation—will buy up that discounted compute for pennies on the dollar.
