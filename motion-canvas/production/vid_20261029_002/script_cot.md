## [CoT Step 1: The Pattern-Interrupt Hook & Core Paradox]

### 1. Formulating 3 Candidate Opening Hooks

* **Candidate 1 (Conventional Financial Drama / Doom Framing):**
  > *"The artificial intelligence boom is barreling toward the biggest financial collapse in modern history. Wall Street thinks tech giants can spend trillions forever, but the bubble is about to burst just like the dot-com crash of 2000."*
  * **Critique & Flaws:** Rejected. This hook relies on exhausted financial media tropes ("biggest financial collapse in history", "bubble about to burst"). More critically, it reinforces the analytically incorrect "Dot-Com 2.0" analogy that our forensic research dossier explicitly dismantles. It fails to offer any hard empirical data in the opening 5 seconds, sounding like generic algorithmic clickbait.

* **Candidate 2 (Narrow Accounting / Technical Granular Framing):**
  > *"If you read Note 1 of Microsoft and Alphabet's recent SEC Form 10-K filings, you will notice they quietly stretched server depreciation from four years to six years to hide over ten billion dollars in hardware depreciation."*
  * **Critique & Flaws:** Rejected. While forensically accurate and grounded in primary SEC filings, it is far too dry and academic for the first 3 seconds of a vertical mobile video. It requires prior domain knowledge of straight-line corporate depreciation schedules before establishing the broader high-stakes tension, losing 70% of viewers before the first visual card can enter.

* **Candidate 3 (Selected Hook — Cognitive Pattern-Interrupt & Quantitative Paradox):**
  > *"Everyone calls AI a bubble, but almost nobody explains what is actually inflating. Between 2025 and 2026, Microsoft, Meta, Google, and Amazon are spending over three hundred and twenty billion dollars a year on AI data centers. Yet according to Sequoia's famous six-hundred-billion-dollar formula, all pure-play AI software today earns less than forty-five billion—leaving a massive five-hundred-billion-dollar revenue chasm."*

### 2. Forensic Deconstruction of the Selected Hook

The selected hook functions as a 3-stage psychological pattern interrupt:
1. **The 1.74s Mirror (Affirmation):** *"Everyone calls AI a bubble,"* (0.00s–1.74s). The viewer immediately recognizes the cultural meme they've seen on Twitter, Bloomberg, and Reddit.
2. **The 3.30s Pivot (Subversion of Intellectual Sloth):** *"...but almost nobody explains what is actually inflating."* (2.12s–5.04s). In exactly 3.3 seconds, this challenges the viewer's unexamined assumptions. It tells them: you've been repeating a buzzword without understanding the mechanical plumbing. This triggers the *information gap reflex* (Loewenstein, 1994).
3. **The Immediate Hard-Data Payoff:** By 11.18s, the script hits the viewer with `$320 billion a year`, followed at 16.98s by Sequoia's `$600 billion formula` and 21.60s by `<$45 billion pure-play ARR`. Within the first 26 seconds, the core paradox is established not through vague opinion, but through an undeniable **$555B quantitative revenue deficit**.

### Detailed Causal Mechanics Across Beats:
* **Beat 01 → Beat 02 Transition:** The viewer is told there is a $500B+ gap. Their natural counter-argument is: *"Wait, Microsoft Azure grew 29% and AWS grew 19%! How is there a gap?"* Beat 02 directly addresses that counter-argument with the ASC 606 round-tripping teardown.
* **Beat 02 → Beat 03 Transition:** The viewer now understands cloud revenue is circular. The immediate next question is: *"Can't they just keep doing this forever?"* Beat 03 shows the physical breaking point: power-constrained data centers cannot host 700W H100s when 1000W Blackwells produce 25x more tokens from the same substation feed.
* **Beat 03 → Beat 04 Transition:** The viewer thinks: *"Okay, this sounds identical to WorldCom and Cisco in 2000."* Beat 04 shatters that false analogy: Big Tech is funded by operating cash flow (Google search, Meta ads), not high-yield junk bonds. But unlike passive fiber optics, GPUs physically wear out and economically obsolete in 36 months.
* **Beat 04 → Beat 05 Transition:** The conclusion naturally resolves the thesis: Big Tech won't go into Chapter 11, but the capital allocation landscape will reorder. Thin wrapper applications get liquidated; power utilities and systems of record inherit cheap compute.
